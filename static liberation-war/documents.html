Instrument of Surrender

The Instrument of Surrender was signed on 16 December 1971 at the Racecourse Ground in Dhaka, marking the formal surrender of Pakistani forces to the Allied Forces of the Mitro Bahini, made up of the Bangladesh Liberation Forces and the Indian Armed Forces. The signing brought an end to the nine-month war and is commemorated in Bangladesh as Victory Day.

Publication Date16 December 1971
Source InformationBangladesh National Archives. Photograph: Government of India, released under the Government Open Data License – India (GODL), via Wikimedia Commons.
Simla Agreement

The Simla Agreement was a peace treaty signed between India and Pakistan following the 1971 war. It set out the principles that would govern future relations between the two countries, including the Line of Control in Kashmir, and formed part of the broader diplomatic resolution that followed Bangladesh's independence.

Publication Date2 July 1972
Source InformationMinistry of External Affairs, India
Proclamation of Independence

The Proclamation of Independence was adopted by the Mujibnagar Government, formally establishing the legal and constitutional basis for Bangladesh's independence. It retroactively recognized the declaration made on 26 March 1971 and established the framework for the provisional government during the war.

Publication Date10 April 1971
Source InformationMujibnagar Government Records. Photograph: Press Information Department, Government of Bangladesh (public domain), via Wikimedia Commons.
Radio Declaration of Independence

In the early hours of 26 March 1971, a declaration of independence was transmitted by radio from Chittagong, informing the nation and the world that Bangladesh had declared itself independent following the crackdown that began the previous night. This broadcast is regarded as the first formal announcement of independence.

Publication Date26 March 1971
Source InformationIndependent Bangladesh Radio Archives. Photograph: Press Information Department, Government of Bangladesh (public domain), via Wikimedia Commons.
Telegram to the United Nations

During the early months of the war, the Provisional Government of Bangladesh sent telegrams to the United Nations Secretary-General and to foreign governments, appealing for international recognition and humanitarian assistance for the millions of refugees crossing into India.

Publication DateApril 1971
Source InformationUnited Nations Archives
Diplomatic Cable from Dhaka

In early April 1971, diplomatic staff based in Dhaka sent cables to their home government describing the unfolding violence and expressing dissent over the official response to the crisis. These cables are now studied as important firsthand diplomatic records of the period.

Publication Date6 April 1971
Source InformationForeign Ministry Archival Records
Kalurghat Radio Declaration

On 27 March 1971, Major Ziaur Rahman of the Bengal Regiment broadcast a declaration of independence over the Swadhin Bangla Betar Kendra radio station at Kalurghat, near Chittagong, on behalf of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman. The broadcast, made as regional resistance was organizing after the crackdown of 25 March, was rebroadcast over the following days and helped spread news of the resistance across the region and internationally.

Publication Date27 March 1971
